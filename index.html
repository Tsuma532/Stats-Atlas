02 / Understand the structure

How distributions connect

Distribution families are not isolated. Some arise as limits, others through sums, transformations, mixtures, or conditional models.

Connection map: core relationships

Use these as a conceptual map; assumptions and parameterisations matter.

RelationshipConnectionWhy it matters
Limit / approximationBinomial → Poisson (rare-event limit); suitably standardised sums → Normal (CLT)Approximations can simplify inference and computation.
TransformationNormal → Lognormal by exponentiation; Uniform → many distributions by inverse CDFModels skewness and generates random samples.
ConjugacyBeta–Binomial; Gamma–Poisson; Normal–Normal under standard assumptionsBayesian updating can be expressed analytically.
Scale mixturesStudent's t as a Normal scale mixture; many heavy-tailed models use latent scalesRobustness to outliers and latent-variable inference.
Compound modelsNegative Binomial as a Poisson–Gamma mixtureExtra-Poisson variation in count data.
Extreme valuesMaxima of broad classes of distributions converge to GEV families under conditionsFlood, heat, reliability, and risk analysis.
DependenceCopulas join marginal distributions into a joint dependence modelSeparate marginal behaviour from dependence structure.
